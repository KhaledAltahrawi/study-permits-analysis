Chapter 1: The Decade of Growth & The 2023 Peak

Between 2015 and 2023, Canada experienced an unprecedented surge in international student enrollment. Following the 2020 pandemic slowdown, the country rapidly reopened its doors, driving permit issuance from ~527,000 in 2020 to an all-time peak of 1,401,690 in 2023.

Key Insight: The post-pandemic rebound in 2021–2023 drove volumes higher than ever recorded in Canadian immigration history, prompting federal infrastructure and housing capacity debates that catalyzed strict regulatory changes.

Chapter 2: Top Source Countries

A small concentration of countries constitutes the primary backbone of Canada’s international student population. Over the 2015–2026 timeframe, India, China, Nigeria, the Philippines, and France remain the top 5 source nations.

Key Insight: India represents the largest cumulative share of students by a wide margin, but experienced the most immediate and pronounced volumetric decline following the 2024–2025 policy adjustments.

Chapter 3: The "August Rush" & Seasonality

Permit issuance adheres to an extremely rigid seasonal rhythm aligned with Canadian higher education academic intake calendars.

Key Insight: Late summer (August and September) consistently accounts for the highest permit volume each year, corresponding to Fall semester matriculation, while February and March represent historical low periods.

Chapter 4: Measuring the Policy Cap Impact (2024 – 2026)

In early 2024, Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) introduced an intake cap on study permit applications to stabilize growth. The data reveals the decisive impact of these policies:

  • 2024: Permit issuance dropped -19.9% compared to the 2023 peak.
  • 2025: Contraction intensified with a -34.8% decline as provincial attestation letters (PALs) and stricter criteria took full effect.
  • 2026 (YTD through August): Issuance volumes stabilized at 478,690, tracking closely (-2.4%) with the aligned 2025 window.
Key Finding: The empirical data confirms that the federal cap successfully reversed the decade-long surge, cutting annual issuance by over 40% from the 2023 peak and establishing a stable new intake baseline in 2026.
